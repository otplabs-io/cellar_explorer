## Conflicts

- Gianetto vine age: 40 to 45 years (one Kermit Lynch listing) vs 8 years (2015 sheet).
- Gianetto vineyard size: 1 ha, 2 ha, 0.6 ha across sources, some for other producers.
- Altitude: 250 m, 280 m, or 280 to 350 m.
- Quality: Berry Bros. praises finesse and charm. Vinous calls the vintage uneven, and a Flatiron summary says mixed. Treat it as producer dependent.
- Yields: Vinous says high. A search summary says the Consorzio reported limited Nebbiolo yields. Chiarlo says normal Nebbiolo bunches.
- September: Chiarlo and Berry Bros. describe cool nights, while another critic says September was very warm with little night cooling.
- Soil ages: the disciplinare names Tortonian and Messinian. Wine guides name Tortonian (west) and Helvetian or Serravallian (east). Helvetian and Serravallian are used for the same eastern soils. The two are not exact synonyms in geology, so a sheet should say Helvetian/Serravallian only where the wine guide wording is wanted.
- Soil character of the eastern side: English Wikipedia says compact sandstone. One summary says harder, more calcareous with more limestone and grey marl. Another says poorer, less compact sand and sandstone. The sources disagree on whether it is more or less compact.
- MGA count: 181 (170 vineyards plus 11 communes) from English Wikipedia and my count of the disciplinare list. German Wikipedia and the Wine Society give about 170.
- Minimum aging: English Wikipedia says at least 36 months after harvest with 18 in wood, and 38 months counted from 1 November. The disciplinare says 38 months from 1 November. Use the disciplinare.
- Core communes: English Wikipedia names four core communes plus parts of others. Other references count five or three whole communes. The disciplinare says three are wholly included (Barolo, Castiglione Falletto, Serralunga).
